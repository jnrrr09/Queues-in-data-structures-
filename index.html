Service Desk Queue

A queue is a line: the first to join is the first to be served. Below are the real-world places that depend on that rule, why a queue is the right structure, and a working ticket queue you can run.

Where queues are required

ApplicationWhat waits in the queueWhy a queue
Customer service and banksCustomers holding ticketsFairness: whoever arrived first is served first.
Printer spoolingPrint jobsThe printer is slower than the users sending jobs, so jobs wait in arrival order.

Parking lot waiting listCars waiting for a free slotThe car that waited longest gets the next free space.

Why queues are necessary

Fairness by construction. Order of arrival is the only rule, so no one can be skipped. Rate matching. When items arrive faster than they can be handled, a queue buffers them instead of dropping them. Decoupling. The side that adds work and the side that does it never need to wait for each other. Speed. Joining at the rear (enqueue) and leaving from the front (dequeue) both take constant time. A stack would serve the newest first, and a sorted list would be slower and break arrival order.
